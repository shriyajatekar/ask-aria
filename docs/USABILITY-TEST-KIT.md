# Ask Aria: usability test kit

A ready-to-run plan for 3–5 short sessions with people who do this work (commerce analysts, key account managers, or e-commerce leads). The goal is real evidence for the case study: what people understood, where they hesitated, and whether they trusted the answers. Nothing here has been run yet.

---

## 1. What we want to learn

1. Can people get from "a number moved" to "why, and what to do" without help?
2. Do they notice and use the **source line**, **"Show on dashboard"** and **confidence** cues, or skip past them?
3. Do they understand that actions are **previewed and confirmed**, and that this demo **simulates** them?
4. When Aria says it's unsure (a corrected question, "too small to explain", no history), do they trust it more or less?
5. Which of Analyst / KAM / CXO answers feel right for their own role?

## 2. Who to invite

- 3–5 people. Five usually surfaces most of the big problems; three is fine for a portfolio.
- At least 2 who analyse marketplace or quick-commerce data weekly (Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit…). One KAM or account lead if you can.
- Not colleagues who have seen Ask Aria before.
- 30 minutes each, remote is fine (screen share). Record only with permission.

## 3. Before the session

- Open https://ask-aria-dun.vercel.app/ in a fresh browser window (or a private window) so there's no earlier chat history.
- Have this script open on another screen, plus a notes sheet (section 7).
- Decide which role each person signs in as (their real role, if it matches).

## 4. Consent script (read aloud)

> Thanks for helping. I'm testing a design, not you, so there are no wrong answers. Ask Aria is a concept for a commerce analytics assistant. Everything you'll see is made-up demo data, and nothing you do sends or changes anything real. Please think out loud as you go: what you're looking at, what you expect, what confuses you. Is it OK if I record the screen and audio? I'll only use short anonymised quotes, and only with your OK.

## 5. Tasks

Don't use the exact words in brackets; they are for you, so you can tell whether people found the feature themselves. Note the time and whether help was needed.

**Warm-up (2 min).** "Take a look at this dashboard. What would you check first?"

**Task 1: Find a cause (6 min).** "ROAS looks lower than last month. Find out why, using whatever you like on the screen." *(Ask Aria → "Why did ROAS…" or the deep dive icon on the ROAS card → driver tree.)*
- Follow-up: "How sure is Aria about that? How can you tell?" *(confidence label, "How sure is this?", source line)*
- Follow-up: "Can you check that against the dashboard?" *(Show on dashboard)*

**Task 2: Has this happened before? (3 min).** "Has this happened before, and what fixed it last time?" *(history / "Last time")*

**Task 3: Something vague (3 min).** "Ask Aria why one platform is 'bad'." *(clarifying question)* Then: "Ask something it can't know, in your own words." *(honest "not sure")*

**Task 4: Act on it (5 min).** "Make a change you'd consider: a bid, a report, or a message to your team." *(preview → confirm; risky-bid warning if they try a very low bid)*
- Follow-up: "What do you think happened when you confirmed?" *(do they notice "Demo only"?)*

**Task 5: Did it work? (3 min).** "How would you find out whether that change helped?" *("Did it work?" / bell)*

**Task 6 (KAM or CXO only, 3 min).** "Get ready for a client call / leadership update." *(brands → draft client update, or biggest risk)*

**Optional (Hindi speakers).** "Ask the same question the way you'd type it to a colleague." *(Hinglish)*

## 6. After each task (ask quickly)

- "On a scale of 1 to 7, how easy was that?" (single ease question; 7 = very easy)
- "Was anything surprising?"

## 8. Debrief questions (5 min)

1. "Would you trust an answer like this enough to act on it? What would you need to see first?"
2. "What did Aria do that a dashboard can't?"
3. "What was missing or annoying?"
4. "If you could keep one thing, what would it be?"

## 9. Making sense of it

- Within a day, list every problem you saw. For each: how many people hit it, and how badly (blocked / slowed / annoyed).
- Group them under the six conversation principles. That gives you "principle → evidence" pairs for the case study.
- Pick the top three problems to fix, and say which you fixed.

## 10. Reporting it honestly in the portfolio

- Say how many people, their roles, and that it was a concept test on synthetic data.
- Use numbers only as counts ("4 of 5 found the root cause without help"), never percentages from 5 people, and never as "results" of the product.
- Quote people only with permission, without names.
- Report what didn't work too. That is what makes the rest believable.
- Keep "20 minutes → under a minute" labelled as the original estimate. These sessions don't measure it unless you time a before/after task on purpose.
